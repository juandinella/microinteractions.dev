## A grip where the pointer is

The divider is a 1px line inside a 12px hit area, so the place to grab is wider than anything on screen. A grip marks it. Most layouts center the grip on the divider, which works on a short bar but costs a trip on a full-height sidebar, where the pointer reaches the edge wherever it happens to be.

Here the grip appears where the pointer meets the line and moves along it. It shows up under the pointer rather than sliding over from its last position, and it follows with no smoothing, because it marks where a press will land.

The pointer crosses the divider every time it moves between the panes. The grip waits 60ms before appearing, longer than a pointer takes to cross 12px, so passing over doesn't flash it. The cursor changes at once, so the delay costs no feedback. Keyboard focus has no pointer to follow: it shows the grip at the middle of the divider and darkens the grip and the line.

## Pressing and dragging

A press widens the grip from 4px to 6px and darkens it. It changes `width`, because `scale()` would stretch the rounded ends into ovals.

The sidebar follows the pointer 1:1. Its width is the width on press plus the pointer's horizontal travel, so pressing a few pixels off the line doesn't make the divider jump. Pointer capture (`setPointerCapture()`) keeps the drag going when the pointer leaves the handle, and for the length of the drag the root element carries the cursor and `user-select: none`. The cursor shows the only direction left: `w-resize` at the maximum, `e-resize` on a collapsed sidebar.

Escape, a cancelled pointer, or the window losing focus cancel the drag and snap the sidebar back.

## Collapsing

Dragging below half the minimum width collapses the sidebar, and dragging back past that point reopens it at the minimum. Both settle in 240ms on a drawer curve, as do a cancelled drag and the double-click that resets the width. With reduced motion they happen at once, and the grip fades in without growing.

The snap runs in JavaScript. A CSS transition on `grid-template-columns` would be cancelled by the next `pointermove`, and the sidebar would jump. The script animates the gap between the width on screen and the width the pointer asks for, shrinking it to zero and adding it to whatever the pointer does next, so the pointer keeps control and a second snap starts from what is on screen.

The width on screen is derived state: the width you chose, clamped to what the container allows. A drag commits its width only when it ends, so a sidebar collapsed by a drag remembers the width from before it. A container too narrow for that width squeezes the sidebar and gives the width back when there is room again.

The container clips the sidebar to its rounded corners, so the last frames of a collapse stay inside the curve. The handle stops 6px from the left edge, keeping its whole hit area inside the container, and the line hides there, where the container's border already draws the edge. A collapsed sidebar is `inert`, so its contents leave the tab order and the accessibility tree.

## Keyboard and touch

The handle follows the window splitter pattern: a focusable `separator` whose value is the sidebar's width. The arrow keys move it by 12px, or 48px with Shift, and stop at the limits. Home collapses the sidebar, End widens it to the maximum, and Enter toggles between collapsed and the last width. Keyboard changes never animate. Arrows with Alt, Cmd, or Ctrl stay with the browser, where Alt+Left goes back.

Touch has no hover, so the grip stays visible at the middle and the hit area grows to 24px.
