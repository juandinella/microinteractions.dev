## A grip where the pointer is

The divider is a 1px line inside a 12px hit area, so the place to grab is wider than anything on screen. A grip marks it. Most layouts center the grip on the divider, which works on a short bar but costs a trip on a full-height sidebar, where the pointer reaches the edge wherever it happens to be.

Here the grip appears where the pointer meets the line and moves along it. It shows up under the pointer rather than sliding over from its last position, and it follows with no smoothing, because it marks where a press will land.

The pointer crosses the divider every time it moves between the panes. The grip waits 60ms before appearing, longer than a pointer takes to cross 12px, so passing over doesn't flash it. The cursor changes at once, so the delay costs no feedback. Only a mouse that moved shows it: a page scrolled under a still cursor, or a tap on a touch laptop, also enters the handle, and neither means the pointer came to the divider. Keyboard focus has no pointer to follow: it shows the grip at the middle of the divider at once, without the fade, and darkens the grip and the line.

## Pressing and dragging

A press widens the grip from 4px to 6px and darkens it. It changes `width`, because `scale()` would stretch the rounded ends into ovals.

A press becomes a drag after 2px of horizontal travel with a mouse, 10px on touch, so a click or a wobbly tap doesn't nudge the width. From there the sidebar follows the pointer 1:1. Its width is the width at that point plus the pointer's horizontal travel since, so pressing a few pixels off the line doesn't make the divider jump. Grabbing the divider while it snaps stops the snap where it is on screen. Pointer capture (`setPointerCapture()`) keeps the drag going when the pointer leaves the handle, and for the length of the drag the root element carries the cursor and `user-select: none`. The cursor shows the only direction left: `w-resize` at the maximum, `e-resize` on a collapsed sidebar.

Escape, a cancelled pointer, or the window losing focus cancel the drag and snap the sidebar back.

## Collapsing

Dragging below half the minimum width collapses the sidebar, and dragging back past that point reopens it at the minimum. Between the two, the width stays at the 120px minimum, but the edge gives with the pointer, each pixel a little less than the last: about 10px at 100px, 26px just before the collapse. A hard stop at 120px would leave the pointer behind and then shut without warning; the give says the next state is close. Let go there and the edge returns to the minimum.

Collapsing, reopening, the give's return, a cancelled drag, and the double-click that resets the width all settle in 240ms on a drawer curve. With reduced motion they happen at once; the give still follows the pointer, and the grip fades in without growing.

The maximum keeps a hard stop. Past it there is no next state to warn of, and the `w-resize` cursor already marks the limit.

The snap runs in JavaScript. A CSS transition on `grid-template-columns` would be cancelled by the next `pointermove`, and the sidebar would jump. The script animates the gap between the width on screen and the width the pointer asks for, shrinking it to zero and adding it to whatever the pointer does next, so the pointer keeps control and a second snap starts from what is on screen.

The width on screen is derived state: the width you chose, clamped to what the container allows. A drag commits its width only when it ends, so a sidebar collapsed by a drag remembers the width from before it. A container too narrow for that width squeezes the sidebar and gives the width back when there is room again.

The container clips the sidebar to its rounded corners, so the last frames of a collapse stay inside the curve. The handle stops 6px from the left edge, keeping its whole hit area inside the container, and the line hides there, where the container's border already draws the edge. A collapsed sidebar is `inert`, so its contents leave the tab order and the accessibility tree.

## Keyboard and touch

The handle follows the window splitter pattern: a focusable `separator` whose value is the sidebar's width. The arrow keys move it by 12px, or 48px with Shift, and stop at the limits. Home collapses the sidebar, End widens it to the maximum, and Enter toggles between collapsed and the last width. Keyboard changes never animate. Arrows with Alt, Cmd, or Ctrl stay with the browser, where Alt+Left goes back.

Holding Enter toggles once. Its key repeat is ignored, so the sidebar doesn't flicker open and shut, while a held arrow keeps moving the divider.

Touch has no hover, so the grip stays visible at the middle and the hit area grows to 24px. The handle sets `touch-action: pan-y`: a vertical swipe that starts on the divider scrolls the page, and the browser cancels the pointer, which ends the drag and leaves the width as it was. The press look waits 100ms or for the drag to start, so a scroll doesn't flash it.

In forced-colors mode the line and grip are drawn in `CanvasText`, and in `Highlight` when focused.
