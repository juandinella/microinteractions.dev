## The scroll is the state

The carousel keeps one value: the track's scroll position, measured in slides. The worm, the current dot, and the counter are derived from it on every scroll event, so the worm has no animation of its own. It follows a swipe as it happens, and if you let go halfway it stays stretched across both dots.

Its two edges move on separate schedules. In the first half of a swipe the leading edge eases over to the next dot, and in the second half the trailing edge catches up. At rest the worm is 12px; halfway it reaches 36px. Both edges use smoothstep, so the worm holds its full length for a beat in the middle. Without that pause it looked like a pill changing size. The rule reads only the position, so on the way back the other edge leads.

## Drag along the dots

The whole strip is a control. Press anywhere on it and move more than 4px, and the scroll follows the pointer. It closes in with an exponential catch-up, so a press far from the worm glides there instead of jumping. On release it snaps to the nearest dot in 180ms. A press that doesn't move is a tap, which travels in 280ms plus 40ms for each extra dot, so a long jump visibly crawls from dot to dot. Scroll snapping stays off while the strip drives the scroll. A cancelled pointer, such as the page taking over a vertical swipe, commits nothing and settles where the scroll already is.

On hover, a ring behind the dots marks the one nearest the mouse, picked with the same mapping as a tap. It glides between dots in 80ms and keeps following during a drag, so it shows where a release will land. The idea comes from Fluid Functionalism's carousel dots.

## Why the worm isn't a clip-path

The first version clipped a bar the width of the strip to the worm's shape. When the row landed on a half pixel, the dots snapped to the pixel grid and the clip-path didn't, so the worm sat a fraction higher and the gray dot underneath peeked out. The worm is now a box like the dots, with the same radius and `top`, moved with `translateX` and sized with `width`.

To recolor it, set `--wd-fg` on the component or any ancestor. The sizes are `--wd-dot`, `--wd-pill`, `--wd-gap`, and `--wd-ring`. They're registered with `@property` as lengths, so the script reads them in pixels whatever unit you write them in.

## Keyboard, touch, and reduced motion

The strip is one Tab stop. The arrow keys, Home, and End move between slides without animating, and focus lands on the new dot, which announces it. The focus ring is drawn on the worm, so it stretches and moves with it. A swipe is announced through a polite live region once the scroll has been still for 250ms, never mid-swipe, and if focus was on a dot it moves to the new one instead. The slides outside the view stay in the accessibility tree, as in any scroll container, so a screen reader can read through all of them. Each dot's hit area is 24px wide and 40px tall, or 44px on touch, and the strip's padding extends it past both ends. `touch-action: pan-y` leaves vertical page scrolling to the browser.

With reduced motion, taps and snaps jump to the slide and the scrub follows the pointer directly. The ring only fades. The worm still follows the scroll, since that motion comes from your own hand. The current dot is longer than the rest, so the state doesn't depend on color. In forced colors mode the strip draws itself with system colors, since the forced background would otherwise erase the dots and the worm. The component assumes left to right; in `dir="rtl"`, `scrollLeft` runs negative.
