## The stem makes the arrow

The chevron and the stem are separate SVG paths. On hover, the chevron moves 2px in the direction it points while the stem fades in and grows from half to full length, and together they read as an arrow. Only `transform` and `opacity` change. The path data stays fixed because Safari doesn't animate `d` in CSS.

The stem grows from its tail. `transform-box: fill-box` makes it scale around its own bounds, and `transform-origin` pins the tail (`right center` on the previous button, `left center` on the next), so the stem reaches toward the chevron instead of growing from its middle. Both paths share an ease-out curve, and CSS transitions reverse from the current state when the pointer leaves.

Each button is a 44px circle. Keyboard focus shows the arrow immediately, with a focus ring. With reduced motion, the state changes without animating.

## Using it for navigation

Use a `<button>` to change slides and an `<a href="…">` to follow a link. Give each control an `aria-label` that names the action and hide the SVG with `aria-hidden="true"`. In a carousel, point `aria-controls` at the carousel's `id`.

Navigate as soon as the button is activated. The chevron already shows direction at rest, so nobody needs to wait for the morph, and on a touch screen the first tap moves to the next item.
