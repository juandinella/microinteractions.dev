## Ticks that follow the pointer

A table of contents for a long page can shrink to a rail of short ticks, one per section. Here the ticks grow around the pointer, like a dock, so the one you're on stands out from its neighbors.

Each tick's length comes from its distance to the pointer, measured in rows, on a cosine that reaches zero three rows away. The pointer's position counts, not only its row: move it down inside one tick and the tick below starts growing before you get there. Computing the falloff by row looks similar at a glance, but every tick jumps when the pointer crosses into the next row and holds still in between.

## One card for the whole rail

Hovering or focusing a tick shows a card with the section's name and a short description. There is one card for the rail, so moving along it slides the card from tick to tick instead of closing one tooltip and opening another.

The text moves the way the card travels. The old text slides 4px ahead with a 3px blur as it fades, and the new one comes in from 4px behind at the same time, so the card is never empty on the way.

## Timing that waits for intent

The card waits 80ms before it opens, so crossing the rail on the way to the content doesn't flash it. It stays 120ms after the pointer leaves, in case the pointer comes back, and opens at once if it closed less than 400ms ago.

The current section's tick follows the scroll and stays long while nothing is lit, so the rail also shows where you are.

## Keyboard, touch, and reduced motion

Every tick is a link. Tab reaches each one and shows its card at once, and the arrow keys move between them. Escape hides the card. Screen readers get the same text as the card, through the link's description.

A finger can't hover, so on touch the first tap shows the card and a second tap on the same tick follows the link. Each tick's row is 24px tall and spans the rail, the minimum target size, even though the line is 2px.

With reduced motion, the ticks change length at once and the card fades in place instead of traveling.
