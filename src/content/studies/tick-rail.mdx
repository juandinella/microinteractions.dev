## Ticks that follow the pointer

A table of contents for a long page, drawn as one short tick per section. The ticks grow around the pointer like a dock, so the one under it stands out.

A tick's length depends on its distance from the pointer, in rows, on a cosine that reaches rest three rows away. Measuring from the pointer's exact position keeps the neighbors moving inside a row. A falloff by row looks the same at a glance, but every tick jumps when the pointer crosses into the next row and holds still in between.

## One card for the whole rail

Hover or focus a tick and a card shows the section's name and description. The rail has one card, which slides from tick to tick, as the single panel does in [FAQ panel](/faq-panel/). Its text moves the same way: the old line leaves 4px ahead with a 3px blur while the new one comes in from behind.

The card opens once the pointer has stopped moving sideways for 80ms. A pointer crossing the rail on the way to the content keeps moving across it, so it doesn't flash a card, while one moving down the ticks still gets it after 80ms. The card stays 120ms after the pointer leaves. Back within 400ms, it skips the wait: a card still fading out turns back and slides to the new tick, and one that has already gone appears without a fade.

The pointer can move onto the card. A click on it or on a tick goes to the section and closes the card, so it doesn't cover what you came to read. It comes back on the next tick you point at.

The current section's tick follows the scroll. It keeps its color and never gets shorter than 16px, so with the pointer elsewhere on the rail it still stands out by its length, not by color alone.

## Keyboard, touch, and reduced motion

Each tick is a link, and the card's text is its description, so screen readers hear what the card shows. Without JavaScript the ticks still work as links. Up and Down move between ticks, Home and End jump, and Escape hides the card from anywhere on the page. Nothing the keyboard does animates: the lit tick changes color at once, and Enter jumps to the section instead of scrolling there.

On touch, the first tap shows the card, and a second tap on the tick or a tap on the card goes there. A tap anywhere else closes it, but a scroll that starts there doesn't. The hit area is the whole row across the rail, 24px tall or 32px on touch screens, though the line is 2px.

With reduced motion, the ticks change length at once and the card appears and moves without transitions.
