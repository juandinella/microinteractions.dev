## Ticks that follow the pointer

A table of contents for a long page, drawn as one short tick per section. The ticks grow around the pointer like a dock, so the one under it stands out.

A tick's length depends on its distance from the pointer, in rows, on a cosine that reaches rest three rows away. Measuring from the pointer's exact position keeps the neighbors moving inside a row. A falloff by row looks the same at a glance, but every tick jumps when the pointer crosses into the next row and holds still in between.

## One card for the whole rail

Hover or focus a tick and a card shows the section's name and description. The rail has one card, which slides from tick to tick. Its text moves the same way: the old line leaves 4px ahead with a 3px blur while the new one comes in from behind.

The card waits 80ms before opening, so crossing the rail on the way to the content doesn't flash it. It stays 120ms after the pointer leaves and reopens at once within 400ms. The pointer can move onto it, and a click on it goes to the section.

The current section's tick follows the scroll. While nothing is lit, it keeps its color and stays a little longer than the rest.

Recolor the CSS version with `--tkr-tick-color`, `--tkr-lit-color`, and `--tkr-current-color`. `--tkr-rail` sets the rail's width.

## Keyboard, touch, and reduced motion

Each tick is a link, and the card's text is its description, so screen readers hear what the card shows. Without JavaScript the ticks still work as links. Up and Down move between ticks, Home and End jump, and Escape hides the card from anywhere on the page. Nothing the keyboard does animates.

On touch, the first tap shows the card, and a second tap on the tick or a tap on the card goes there. The hit area is the whole row across the rail, 24px tall or 32px on touch screens, though the line is 2px.

With reduced motion, the ticks change length at once and the card appears and moves without transitions.
