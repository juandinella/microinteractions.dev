## A drop made of eight dots

The dot is eight small circles in their own layer, and only that layer goes through an SVG filter. `feGaussianBlur` softens the circles until neighbors overlap. `feColorMatrix` multiplies the alpha by 18 and subtracts 7, so anything under about 39% opacity disappears and anything above about 44% turns solid. Circles that share a blurred edge fuse into one outline, and `feComposite` with `atop` paints their sharp color inside it.

At rest, all eight circles sit on top of each other and read as a regular radio dot. When the selection changes, the head and the tail travel on the same ease-in-out curve, the tail 60ms behind. The six circles between them spread out and shrink toward the tail, so the outline tapers into a drop instead of a dumbbell. The longer the drop stretches, the thinner its tail gets, down to 30% of the dot.

The rings sit outside the filter and stay sharp. The old ring lets go as the tail leaves it and the new one darkens as the head arrives. Each label changes with its ring, so a choice reads as one event.

## Keyframes from a function

A delayed CSS transition could lag the tail, but it couldn't taper the drop by the distance between head and tail. The script samples the head and tail at 61 points along the motion, computes every circle's position and scale, and gives each circle its own keyframes through the Web Animations API.

The resting position stays in CSS. One `:has()` rule per option sets `--goo-index`, so the dot marks the selection before the script runs. A new choice mid-pour finds the head and tail at the running animation's current time and starts the next drop from there. That drop uses ease-out, because ease-in-out would stall a moving drop at zero velocity before accelerating again.

When nothing is checked yet, the first choice has nowhere to pour from, so the dot swells into its ring. It grows by scale alone, since the filter's alpha cut would turn a fade into a pop.

To recolor the CSS version, set `--goo-fg`, `--goo-ring-color`, or `--goo-muted` on the fieldset or any ancestor. Each group gets its own filter `id`, and `isolation: isolate` keeps the drop under the rest of the page's UI, such as a sticky header.

## Keyboard, touch, and reduced motion

Native radios make the group one Tab stop and move the selection with the arrow keys. The focus ring wraps the whole row through `label:has(:focus-visible)`. Arrow keys can repeat faster than the drop travels, so keyboard changes skip the drop and switch at once.

Safari stops matching `:focus-visible` once the arrow keys move focus to the next radio. The script sets `data-keyboard` on a key press and clears it on the next pointer press, and CSS treats `:focus` inside it as keyboard focus.

A tap works like a click, and the whole row is the hit area. With reduced motion, the dot moves without the drop. The selected option always has a dot inside its ring, so the state doesn't depend on color.
